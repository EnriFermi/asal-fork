## Secondary claims that also need support or softening

| Claim | Problem | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| **“NN-based OEE metrics are black boxes and cannot be connected to physical theories of complexity.”** | Too absolute. Some learned metrics may be interpretable post-hoc or linked to information/geometric quantities. | Soften to “are often black-box and do not by construction encode a physical complexity principle.” Cite ASAL/Leniabreeder accurately. |
| **“MSPD is a temporal extension of MSSC / inherits RG architecture intact.”** | This is methodological, not purely empirical. “Inherits intact” is strong because time windows are not literally Kadanoff spatial coarse-graining. | Provide derivation: spatial scale \(\lambda\) in MSSC \(\mapsto\) temporal observation window \(W\); cross-scale dissimilarity in image space \(\mapsto\) trajectory-distribution space. Use “analogous to” unless you formalize a genuine RG map. |
| **“Lagrangian tracking avoids Eulerian velocity-grid noise and yields stable distributions.”** | Needs ablation or should be phrased as motivation. | Compare Lagrangian vs Eulerian estimates under same simulation: variance across seeds, sensitivity to grid resolution, signal/noise for C2. |
| **\(\Delta H(W,\tau)\ge 0\).** | As written, \(\Delta H=\mathbb{E}_{i\ne j}\mathrm{SW}_1(p_i,p_j)-\tilde H_0\) is not mathematically guaranteed nonnegative. | Either define \(\Delta H_+=\max(\mathbb{E}\mathrm{SW}_1-\tilde H_0,0)\), or remove \(\ge0\), or prove the shuffle baseline is always lower, which is unlikely. |
| **“High MSPD is a signature of hierarchical complexity.”** | Currently definitional/interpretive. Could be gamed by non-hierarchical temporal variation. | Add synthetic controls: uniform field, white noise, single periodic glider, random drifting particles, multi-speed but non-interacting particles, genuinely hierarchical ecology. Show MSPD peaks in the intended regime. |
| **“MSPD applies to any simulation that exposes particle trajectories.”** | Too broad; stationarity, sampling rate, internal degrees of freedom, and meaningful velocity distributions matter. | Phrase as “directly applicable to simulations with meaningful particle trajectories under a calibrated \(\tau\) range.” |
| **“MSPD recovers two Vanchurin criteria.”** | Scale separation is recovered by MSPD; frustration is recovered by the two-metric analysis, and specifically appears on the NN snapshot axis. | Rewrite: “MSPD recovers scale separation; when paired with a snapshot metric, the resulting analysis detects frustration in MSPD-optimized systems.” |
| **“MSPD optimization produces frustration independently/as a consequence.”** | Needs causal comparison: high-MSPD systems vs random controls vs NN-OEE controls; otherwise it is correlation. | Show frustration gap is elevated in MSPD-opt relative to matched random controls. For causal language, use multiple seeds and matched optimization budgets. |
| **“Snapshot and dynamic complexity are orthogonal axes.”** | 7/7 vs 3/7 only shows different frustration response, not orthogonality. | Add direct correlation/independence test between \(D_P\) and NN snapshot metric across many systems. Otherwise say “empirically dissociable axes.” |
| **“Multi-objective evolution against MSPD and snapshot metric will dominate either driver alone.”** | This is a prediction, not a result. | Mark explicitly as hypothesis/future work unless you run multi-objective experiments. |
| **“Frustration remains inaccessible to dynamic-only optimisation.”** | Too strong. Your data can show “not captured by MSPD in our assay”, not impossibility. | Soften to “not reliably detected by the MSPD dynamic-axis measure in our current experiments.” |

## The claims I would prioritize

The minimum defensible paper needs **C1, C2, C3, C4, C5**. C6 can remain preliminary, but only if phrased narrowly.

The most important dependency is methodological: before results, §3.3, §3.4, §3.5, and §3.6 must be precise. Without these, C3 and C5 are not testable claims. In particular, the paper currently cannot support “system-identified \(\tau^*\)” until §3.3 defines the selection rule, and cannot support “frustration is structural” until §3.6 defines the frustration gap and statistical test.

The highest-risk claim is **C5**. To make it publishable, I would avoid saying “orthogonal” unless you actually show low correlation/independence between the two metrics. With only the current 7/7 vs 3/7 evidence, the safer claim is:

> Frustration is reliably detected by the snapshot structural metric but not by MSPD in our current dynamic-axis assay, indicating that the two metrics capture empirically dissociable aspects of complexity.

The second highest-risk claim is **C6**. As written, Boids are not yet evidence. Either run Boids or remove Boids from the abstract-level claim.
